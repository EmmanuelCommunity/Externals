A word of welcome

“Emmanuel” means God with us. It is the name a small prayer group in Paris received in the early 1970s, after waiting on the Holy Spirit, and it is everything we hope to share with you here: God is near, he loves you, and he invites you to live that love alongside others.

Whether we have met before or this is our first hello, welcome. Read what speaks to you, pray with the questions, and, if you would like company on the way, step into our online group at the end of this page.

In Christ,
Paul & Anne Perez
U.S. Coordinators, Emmanuel Community

Who we are

The Emmanuel Community is a Catholic community of lay people and clergy: married couples, singles, priests, and consecrated men and women who share one call, to grow in holiness and to live their faith in everyday life.

It was born in Paris in 1972 out of a prayer group of the Catholic Charismatic Renewal, and today it is present in about sixty countries. In the United States we are young and small, and we are learning to love small beginnings. Here is the story in a few dates.

Adoration. Compassion. Evangelization.

Three words describe how Emmanuel lives. They are not three programs but one movement of love: we receive it in front of the Lord, we let it open our hearts to those who suffer, and we carry it to others.

Adoration › Compassion › Evangelization

Adoration

Time in silent love before Jesus

Adoration is simply being with Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament. For Emmanuel it is the root of everything. The founder, Pierre Goursat, was first of all an adorer. In Rwanda the Community grew from a home chapel where the Eucharist was kept.

Jesus asked St. Margaret Mary for a weekly Holy Hour on Thursday night, to keep him company and console his Heart.

Try this weekFind a parish with Eucharistic adoration. Sit for fifteen minutes and say only: “Lord, here I am.”

Compassion

Love that is moved to act

Time before the Lord teaches us to see as he sees: the lonely, the wounded, the forgotten. In Rwanda that became a center that feeds and educates street children, begun in 1992 and still serving, and a place where anyone who is tired can come to be heard and find peace.

Here, compassion usually looks smaller and closer: a neighbor, a meal, a visit, a phone call.

Try this weekChoose one work of mercy within reach: feed someone, visit someone who is alone, or bear a small wrong patiently.

Evangelization

Sharing the good news, in joy

Pierre Goursat called evangelization the lifeblood of the community. A prayer group that turned inward in the early 1970s faded away, while the groups that reached out kept growing.

It is not about being perky or persuasive. It is saying, in your own words and from your own ups and downs, that God loves people. And it changes the evangelizer first: many of those who go out say they began by not wanting to.

Try this weekTell one person, simply, “God loves you,” or offer a word of encouragement.

From our gathering

In October, families from across the country gathered in Pennsylvania for a weekend of praise, adoration, teaching and friendship. The theme was God’s love poured out through the Holy Spirit, with a special focus on the Sacred Heart of Jesus.

The relics of St. Margaret Mary Alacoque (1647–1690), who received the revelations of the Sacred Heart at Paray-le-Monial, were with us all weekend, and many spent quiet time praying near them. The Community has a special bond with Paray-le-Monial: Pierre Goursat chose it as the center of the Community.

From our teachings

Four short pieces drawn from talks given at our gathering. Open any of them, and take the questions into prayer.

The Spirit who never stoppedHow Emmanuel began, and why listening matters

At Pentecost Jesus promised to send his Spirit, and the Spirit has kept guiding the Church ever since. Around 1900 a sister kept writing to Pope Leo XIII, urging him to entrust the new century to the Holy Spirit. In January 1901 he did.

That same month, Protestants in Topeka, Kansas prayed for the Spirit and received him as at Pentecost. In 1967 Catholic students at Duquesne University asked Protestant friends to pray with them, and they too received an outpouring. From that stream came a small prayer group in Paris, which waited on the Spirit before choosing a name. Two of them, independently, sensed the same one: Emmanuel, God with us.

One lesson stays with us. A prayer meeting was told, again and again, to go out and evangelize. It said yes, and never went. That meeting faded away. The Spirit speaks, and our part is to listen and then to do what he asks.

ReflectIs there something I have sensed God inviting me to do? What have I done with it?
A Heart that could not hold backSt. Margaret Mary and the love poured out

Scripture links the pierced Heart of Jesus to the gift of the Holy Spirit: rivers of living water (John 7), blood and water flowing out immediately (John 19), fire on the earth (Luke 12), tongues of fire at Pentecost (Acts 2), and God’s love poured into our hearts (Romans 5:5).

In 1673, before the Blessed Sacrament at Paray-le-Monial, St. Margaret Mary rested her head on the chest of Jesus. He told her that his Heart could no longer contain its love for people, for each one and “for you in particular.” He asked for a feast in honor of his Heart, for Communion on the First Friday of each month, and for a Holy Hour on Thursday night.

One reflection at our gathering wondered whether this was, in its own way, an outpouring of the Holy Spirit: fire and love, a personal encounter, a call to mission. It was offered as a reflection, not as Church teaching, and as an invitation to ask what the same love might awaken in us.

ReflectJesus says his love is for each of us “in particular.” What happens in me when I let him say that to me?
Small beginnings: the Community in RwandaWhat thirty people and a patient yes can do

In 1990 about thirty brothers and sisters gathered in Kigali for the first weekend of the Emmanuel Community in Rwanda. Behind it was no strategy, only a wife’s patient prayer, a husband’s conversion, a friendship and an encounter. Cyprien and Daphrose Rugamba had met the Community in France. Adoration was the root, compassion became a center for street children, and evangelization took them into streets, bars and open-air markets.

Cyprien worked for unity during years of deep division. On April 7, 1994, the first day of the genocide against the Tutsi, he and Daphrose were killed in their home, with six of their ten children, after a night of adoration. The Church has opened the cause for their beatification.

Nobody expected the Community to survive. It did, and today it counts about 1,800 members in Rwanda. Its message to us is simple: do not fear small beginnings.

ReflectWho around me is tired, divided, or far from God? What small, concrete step could I take toward them?
Good news in seven linesA simple way to share what you have received

The kerygma (Greek for “the proclamation”) is the heart of the good news. It is not a script, but a map for when you wonder what to say, and it always starts with the first line. For Catholics it never stops at words: it leads people to meet Jesus in his Church, in Scripture and the sacraments, above all the Eucharist.

  1. God our Father loves you and has a plan for your life.
  2. Sin wounds our friendship with God and with one another.
  3. Jesus, the Son of God, died and rose again to save us, and he offers us mercy.
  4. Repent and believe in the Gospel: turn back to God and receive his forgiveness in the sacrament of Reconciliation.
  5. In Baptism and Confirmation we are born into God’s family and sealed with the Holy Spirit.
  6. Abide in Christ and his Body, the Church: he feeds us in the Eucharist and in his Word, and we grow together in prayer.
  7. Go forth and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19).

The Catechism (2472) reminds us that every Christian, by the Baptism and Confirmation they have received, is called to witness to the faith in words and in deeds. The deeds are the works of mercy:

Spiritual

  • Instruct the ignorant
  • Counsel the doubtful
  • Admonish sinners
  • Forgive injuries
  • Bear wrongs patiently
  • Pray for the living and the dead

Corporal

  • Feed the hungry
  • Give drink to the thirsty
  • Shelter the homeless
  • Clothe the naked
  • Visit the imprisoned
  • Bury the dead
ReflectWhich line could I share with someone this week, in my own words? Which work of mercy is within reach for me right now?
